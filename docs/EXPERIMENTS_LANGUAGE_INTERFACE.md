# Experiments for the language-as-interface paper

Paper: "Language as the Interface of Power: Co-Constitution, the Expression and Utility Gaps, and AI Safety in the Digital Language Divide". Proposition P1 (safety gradient) is the target. Status of everything below: planned, nothing here has been run for this paper.

## Reuse from the earlier health-domain study
Code, configs and rendering methods were copied from the earlier study (`docs/source_*.md`, `results/source_paper/`). That study already has behavioral results over 27 conditions (SAE, 5 dialects, 3 translation levels, 18 code-switch conditions) and six models, in `results/aggregate/` (`exp1_*` harmful compliance and false refusal, `exp2_*` confidence). They are the behavioral anchor for P1: harmful compliance was higher than on SAE under dialects, translation and code-switching for the five instruction-tuned models (see `results/source_paper/SUMMARY.md`). Caveats carried over: renderings are synthetic, no native-speaker validation, automated judge, seven code-switch conditions above the 15% unrendered flag, health-domain prompts only.

Blinding: that study is under double-blind review. Do not cite its results in the manuscript in a way that identifies authors, and do not make either repository public until review ends.

## E1. Refusal and harmfulness directions under dialect and code-switching (mechanism)
Question: do dialect rewrites and code-switched inputs shift where prompts sit relative to the harmfulness and refusal directions, beyond what standard-language translation does?

Prior work (wording checked against the 2026-11-19 verification; details in "Literature by stage" below): Arditi et al. 2024 (single refusal direction); Zhao et al. 2025, NeurIPS 2025 (harmfulness read at the last instruction token and refusal at the post-instruction token are distinct); Wang et al. 2025, NeurIPS 2025 (refusal vectors near-parallel across 14 standard languages; Yoruba is weakest in safety alignment, not in harmful-versus-harmless separation); Zhang 2026, MRL (a control direction matched the refusal cosine, so a raw cosine needs a control); Oppong et al. 2026, preprint (11 of 12 language-model pairs in Twi, Hausa, Amharic and Swahili keep under 10% of the English signal at the final input token, the exception being Swahili on Llama at about 27%; correlational). None of these measures directions under dialect rewrites or code-switching; Minionese reports its subspace geometry for standard translation only.

Design:
1. Models: open-weight models already served in this repo (Gemma-3-27B, Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-Small-3.2-24B; medical variants optional). Record versions.
2. Directions: difference in means on English harmful versus matched benign prompts, at a layer and token position chosen on a held-out English split (record the choice before running other conditions). Separate harmfulness direction (Zhao: harmfulness-labelled prompts) and refusal direction (refused versus complied).
3. Inputs: the same items rendered in SAE, 5 dialects, 3 translations, 9 English-base and 9 native-base code-switch conditions (reuse `scripts/02*` rendering and the quality gates).
4. Measures per condition: projection on each direction; retention of the English harmful-versus-benign separation (`mechanism.retention`); refusal rate from the existing generation pipeline; cosine to the English direction reported beside a random control (`mechanism.random_unit`) and a non-safety control direction.
5. Comprehension check: for each rendered input, back-translation or restatement (existing `scripts/02q_backtranslate.py` pattern), so a fall in projection is not read as a failure to understand.
6. Causal check (secondary): ablate the English direction (`mechanism.ablate`) and steer, then compare refusal change across conditions.
7. Reading: harmfulness projection falls with comprehension intact = recognition gap. Harmfulness holds but refusal falls = recognition-to-refusal gap. Report descriptively with bootstrap intervals over items. No significance tests across the grid; no model ranking.

Limits to state: open-weight models only; synthetic renderings; English-derived directions may be the wrong reference for other varieties; one layer choice; health-domain prompts unless a second source is added.

Built and tested (offline, on a tiny random Llama with a stub tokenizer, plus synthetic arrays; no real model has been run):
- `src/dlsd/mechanism.py`: directions, projection, retention, controls, ablation, held-out layer choice, per-condition summary (`tests/test_mechanism.py`).
- `src/dlsd/activations.py` and `scripts/10_extract_activations.py`: residual activations, one shard per model and condition, resumable. Shard keys: `L<l>` post-instruction (last prompt) token, unchanged; added (default, `--last-only` restores the old behaviour) `I<l>` last token of the user instruction, `M<l>` mean over user-content tokens (template tokens excluded), per-token embedding norms of the user content (`emb_norms`, `emb_offsets`), `span_exact`, `n_layers`, `capture_version`. An older last-token-only shard is recomputed and replaced through a temporary file. Tests check that a prompt gets the same activation alone or in a batch, that `L<l>` equals the earlier extraction, and that `I<l>` and `M<l>` match a manual forward pass.
- `scripts/11_directions.py`: picks the layer on the English baseline only (held-out split), fits the direction on half A, then summarises every condition (retention, AUROC on the English direction, cosine to English and to a random control). Writes `results/aggregate/e1_directions_<model>.csv/json`.
- `src/dlsd/tokenization.py` and `scripts/12_tokenization.py`: tokenization covariates per prompt (tokens per word, premium against the item's English text, byte-fallback share, share of words split into 3 or more pieces) and a decomposition of each condition's projection gap into a part explained by log token premium and a remainder (within-condition slope, bootstrap over items). Tested on synthetic data; script is glue and has not been run with a real tokenizer.
- Stages 2 to 8 of the stage map below: `src/dlsd/representation.py`, `src/dlsd/langprobe.py`, `src/dlsd/harm_refusal.py`, `scripts/13` to `18` (`tests/test_stage_map.py`, including an end-to-end run of the six scripts on synthetic shards).
Not built: the causal check (ablate and steer during generation) and the non-safety control direction.

### Stage map (added 2026-11-19)
One measurable per stage, from tokenization to output. Status for every row: built and tested offline (tiny random Llama with a stub tokenizer, synthetic arrays and synthetic shards); untested on real models. `<l>` is a block index; `baseline|SAE` is the English reference throughout.

| Stage | What happens | Measure | Script (writes `results/aggregate/`) | Literature (verified) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Tokenization | Text becomes subword tokens; dialect and code-switched text may fragment | Tokens per word, premium against the item's English prompt, byte-fallback share, share of words split into 3 or more pieces; gap decomposition on log premium | `12_tokenization.py` (`e1_tokenization_*`) | Petrov et al. 2023; Lundin et al. 2026; Kanjirangat et al. 2025; Geh et al. 2025; Li, Wu and Liu 2026 | Built, untested on real models |
| 2 Embedding | Each token is looked up in the embedding matrix (`hidden_states[0]`) | Per prompt: mean content-token embedding norm and share of tokens above the English percentile; per condition: gap in mean norm from English, raw and adjusted for log premium (fragmentation-aware) | `13_embedding.py` (`e1_embedding_*`, `e1_embedding_gaps_*`) | No verified source; exploratory | Built, untested on real models |
| 3 Early-layer assembly | Early layers assemble multi-token words | Paired cosine of mean-pooled early-layer states, rendered prompt against the same item's English prompt, beside the English baseline (same item, SAE against the English prompt: 1.0 by construction, no paraphrase involved) and a mismatched-item floor; `normalized` = position between floor and 1 | `14_early_assembly.py` (`e1_early_assembly_*`) | Kaplan et al. 2025; Feucht et al. 2024 (word assembly only, nothing about refusal) | Built, untested on real models |
| 4 Language and variety identification | Language or variety identity becomes linearly readable | Seeded, item-grouped, cross-validated logistic probe per layer on mean-pooled and last-token states; accuracy by layer and condition beside a shuffled-label control, chance and the majority-class rate; code-switched conditions: share of rows given each label | `15_language_probe.py` (`e1_langprobe_*`, `e1_langprobe_codeswitch_*`) | Sallouh et al. 2026; Upadhyaya and Sikdar 2026; Minionese (routing is a hypothesis there, not a measurement) | Built, untested on real models |
| 5 Semantic representation | Mid layers carry meaning that may be shared across languages | Paired cosine and linear CKA of mid-layer mean-pooled states against English, each beside a mismatched-item floor; comprehension check hook (restatement scores joined by item and condition, `02q` pattern), similarity reported for understood and not-understood items | `16_semantic.py` (`e1_semantic_*`) | Minionese (geometry, standard translation only); Wang et al. 2025 | Built, untested on real models |
| 6 Harmfulness | Harmfulness is read at the last instruction token (t_inst) | Harmfulness retention (share of the English harmful-versus-benign separation kept at `I<l>`), with item bootstrap interval, and AUROC | `17_harm_refusal.py` (`e1_harm_refusal_*`); direction arithmetic in `mechanism.py`, layer choice in `11_directions.py` | Zhao et al. 2025; Oppong et al. 2026; Aziz, Hanif and Koto 2026 | Built, untested on real models |
| 7 Refusal | Refusal is read at the post-instruction token (t_post-inst) | Refusal direction (English refused minus complied harmful prompts, at `L<l>`); refusal position per condition (English complied = 0, refused = 1) with interval; refusal rate; AUROC of refused against complied within condition; cosine of the harmfulness and refusal directions | `17_harm_refusal.py` | Zhao et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025; Zhang 2026 | Built, untested on real models |
| 8 Output | The model answers, refuses, or answers in another language | Per condition: refusal rate, share of responses in English, in the prompt language and empty, and mean projections by refusal label and by response language (`checks.detect`); aggregates only | `18_output_join.py` (`e1_output_*`) | LinguaMap (Tamo et al. 2026); Aswal and Jaiswal (CMP-RT); Haq and Saldías 2026 | Built, untested on real models |

Reading across stages (descriptive, bootstrap intervals over items, no significance tests across the grid): a condition that keeps harmfulness (stage 6) with comprehension intact (stage 5) but has a lower refusal position (stage 7) shows a recognition-to-refusal gap; one that loses harmfulness while comprehension holds shows a recognition gap; a gap that tracks premium (stages 1 and 2) or early-layer similarity (stage 3) points to fragmentation upstream. Language decodability (stage 4) is reported as decodability only.

Order of runs: `10` with early, middle and late layers (for example `--layers 1,2,4,8,12,16,20,24,28`; the author picks), `11` (layer choice on English), `12`, then `13` to `18`. Refusal labels for `17` and `18` come from the judge labels of the existing generations (`results/scored/scored_exp1.jsonl`, row-level, local only, read in memory and never written out; `--scored` accepts other paths, for example the gate-failed sensitivity file).

### Literature by stage (corrected per the 2026-11-19 verification)
Only claims the verification confirmed or corrected are used. Stage 1: Petrov et al. (NeurIPS 2023) report that the same text can differ in tokenized length by up to 15 times in some cases, a maximum rather than a typical premium. Lundin et al. (AfricaNLP 2026 workshop) find, over 16 languages and 10 models, that higher fertility predicts lower accuracy. Kanjirangat et al. (EMNLP 2025) find tokenization parity predicts span tasks and information parity predicts semantic tasks on dialectal data. Geh, Shao and Van den Broeck (ACL 2025) show that re-tokenizing a harmful request is a competitive jailbreak, with no layer analysis in the abstract. Li, Wu and Liu (arXiv 2607.01239, no venue) locate the safety disruption from space-insertion fragmentation in the last 30% of layers, on 50 English HarmBench prompts. Stages 2 and 3: Kaplan et al. (ICLR 2025) place detokenization mainly in early and middle layers, and Feucht et al. (EMNLP 2024) find early-layer erasure at the last token of multi-token words in English only; neither says anything about refusal, and no checked source shows an early-layer mechanism for a safety failure. Stage 4: Minionese (Ifebi, Kong and Mehrotra, 2026; a workshop paper, cited by its arXiv venue line as TAIGR at ICML 2026, not the ICML main track) hypothesizes that code-switching works by disrupting language-identification routing and lists testing it as future work; it has no internal language-ID probe. Sallouh, Popović and Färber (EMNLP 2026) decode Arabic dialect identity above chance but low in absolute terms (0.15 to 0.25 against 3.84% chance, on mGPT and BLOOM). Upadhyaya and Sikdar (arXiv 2608.29936) find late-layer safety features entangled with language identity in Llama and Qwen, less in Gemma, with cross-lingual sharing peaking in middle layers except for Qwen. Stage 5: Minionese reports principal angles to the English harmfulness subspace of 40 to 65 degrees in Tier 1 (depending on model) and about 85 to 90 degrees in its lowest tier (Tier 4) only, with no ranges stated for Tiers 2 and 3, for standard translation only, and calls the link to behaviour correlational. Stages 6 and 7: Zhao et al. (NeurIPS 2025) read harmfulness at t_inst, the last token of the user instruction, and refusal at t_post-inst, the last post-instruction template token; on Llama3-Instruct-8B harmfulness steering peaks at layer 9 and refusal steering at layer 11. Wang et al. (NeurIPS 2025) find refusal vectors near-parallel across 14 languages and weaker harmful-versus-harmless separation outside English; Yoruba is weakest in safety alignment (baseline compliance) but not on separation; the near-or-above-90% compliance after ablation is reported for vectors derived from German, Chinese and Thai, while the English-derived vector gives a "substantial increase". Oppong et al. (arXiv 2608.11146) measure one English mean-difference projection at the final input token, which does not by itself separate harm recognition from refusal. Aziz, Hanif and Koto (arXiv 2606.01196) report harmful-prompt refusal of 87.9%, 78.9% and 43.9% for high-, mid- and low-resource languages while a pooled harmfulness score keeps AUC mostly above 0.85; their random direction controls the classification result only, not steering. Zhang (MRL 2026) found a control direction matching the refusal cosine and a pre-registered transfer criterion met in 0 of 9 languages. Minionese supports "encoded but not refused" for its Tier 3 only; for Tier 4 it reports probe AUC approaching chance (collapse of the harm representation), so Tier 4 is not evidence of "understood but not refused". Stage 8: LinguaMap (ICLR 2026) reports language consistency on Qwen-3-32B falling from 45.17% to 8.35% while accuracy rises. CMP-RT (Aswal and Jaiswal, preprint) keeps relevance for ChatGPT and Llama but not for Gemma (0.65) or Mistral (0.74), and does not characterise response language. Haq and Saldías (FAccT 2026) report refusal of 28.9% for an explicit profile against 5.4% for AAVE (Table 4). Minionese: code-switching remains effective in Tier 4 while transliteration is near zero there. IndicJR's remark that refusals to Indic prompts are sometimes in English is a qualitative audit note, not a measured rate, and is not cited as a finding. Arditi et al. 2024 and Rust et al. 2021 were not part of the verification; Rust et al. is kept only as the source of the fertility definition.

### Open decisions for the author
1. Layers to extract in `10` (stages 3 and 4 need early and per-layer coverage; the current command takes only the layers given).
2. Whether the refusal direction (refused minus complied) is fit at the layer chosen in `11` for the harmfulness direction, or chosen separately on English for each position (Zhao et al. find different best layers for the two).
3. Whether "partial" judge labels count as refusal.
4. Stage 3 ceiling: the SAE prompt is the reference, so SAE against itself is 1.0 by construction. A non-trivial ceiling would need an English paraphrase condition, which does not exist; adding one changes the conditions and needs your decision.
5. Comprehension scores exist only for the three translation conditions (`02q` does not back-translate code-switched renderings or dialects). Restatement for dialect and code-switch conditions needs a new source.
6. Stage 4 trains on monolingual conditions only; code-switched conditions are scored by label share, without a correct label.
7. Response language uses `checks.detect` restricted to en, es, hi, sw, so a reply in another language is forced into one of these.
8. The user-content span is found by tokenizing the template prefix separately; `span_exact` is recorded per prompt. Check its share on each real tokenizer before reading stages 2 to 5.

### Tokenization (added 2026-11-19)
Dialect and code-switched text fragments into more and rarer tokens, so a lower projection could reflect fragmentation rather than language or variety. Petrov et al. 2023 report that the same text can differ in tokenized length by up to 15 times in some cases (a maximum, not a typical premium), and Lundin et al. 2026 find that higher fertility predicts lower accuracy across 16 languages and 10 models. Minionese hypothesizes that code-switching disrupts language-identification routing but does not measure it. E1 therefore reports, per model's own tokenizer, the measures above, the decomposition, and a controlled contrast (same meaning, different tokenization: spelling-normalized dialect rewrites, native versus romanized script). Reading rule: if a condition's gap shrinks toward zero after adjustment, say it is largely a tokenization effect; if it persists, say it is not explained by fragmentation. The adjustment extrapolates when token premium barely varies within a condition; report the slope and its interval.
Zhao et al. 2025 read the harmfulness direction at the last instruction token and the refusal direction at the post-instruction token. The instruction-token position is now captured (`I<l>`, stage map above).

## E2. Behavioral register pilot (cheap, run first)
Hold intent and harm level fixed; vary register only. Reuse the existing generation and scoring pipeline. Add a benign-helpfulness arm (utility gap): answer quality and false refusals by register, not only harmful compliance. Source prompts beyond health (for example a general harmful-request set plus matched benign) so the claim is not domain-bound; choose and record the source before running.

## E3. Feedback-loop simulation (optional, speculative)
Simulate who is counted in preference data by variety, then measure drift of a model fitted on the counted data toward the counted variety. Label as simulation, not evidence about deployed systems.
